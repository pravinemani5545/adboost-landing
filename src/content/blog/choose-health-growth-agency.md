Hiring the wrong agency costs a health brand more than the retainer. It costs the 90 days of runway spent learning your category on your budget, the ad account trust burned by policy violations, and sometimes the account itself.

We're an agency, so discount our bias however you like — but we've onboarded enough partners *after* a generic-agency failure that the pattern is unmistakable. Here are the nine questions that would have saved those founders a quarter, and what good answers sound like.

## 1. "How many health brands are in your current portfolio?"

Not "have you worked with," which invites a story about one supplement client in 2023. *Current portfolio.* Health advertising policy changes quarterly; an agency needs live pattern data across accounts to know what's being enforced this month. A generic shop learns each policy change on your account, one rejection at a time.

## 2. "Walk me through your compliance review process — before launch."

The generic answer is "we're careful with claims." The real answer names a process: every creative reviewed against Meta, Google, and TikTok health-vertical policy *before* launch; claims mapped to substantiation; landers audited as part of the ad. If you're in GLP-1 or prescription-adjacent categories, follow up with certification specifics — LegitScript, platform written permissions. (Our own checklist is public: [the GLP-1 compliance playbook](/blog/glp1-advertising-compliance-2026/).)

## 3. "What CAC ranges do you see for brands like mine?"

A specialist quotes numbers unprompted — by vertical, by channel, with the caveats that make them honest. A generalist deflects with "every brand is different." Every brand *is* different, but if an agency can't tell you [what telehealth brands actually pay per patient](/blog/telehealth-cac-benchmarks-2026/), they have no baseline to judge your account against.

## 4. "How many creative variants will you produce monthly — and who makes them?"

The single most predictive operational question. Creative velocity is the dominant CAC lever in constrained health categories, and "we'll work with your assets" means the pipeline is your problem. Look for a concrete number (our standard is 20+ variants per partner per month, produced in-house), an angle library, and a named process for [passing health-vertical review](/blog/health-ad-creative-rejected-fix/).

## 5. "What happens in the first 30 days?"

Generic agencies say "learning phase" — which too often means your budget funds their education. Specialists have a template because they've run it dozens of times: measurement rebuilt first, compliance audit, creative pipeline live in week one, directional movement inside two weeks. Ours is public on the site: set up in 5 days, compounding results by day 31. Whatever the agency's version is, it should be specific enough to hold them to.

## 6. "Show me a full-funnel result, not a ROAS screenshot."

Platform ROAS screenshots are the agency equivalent of before/after photos. Ask instead for a story with a mechanism: what was broken, what changed, in what order, over what timeline. "We took a partner from 0.8 to 3.5 ROAS in 60 days" only means something when it's followed by *how* — creative system, funnel rebuild, attribution fix — and what happened after the case study window ended.

## 7. "Which metrics will you report, and which will you be accountable to?"

Weak agencies report platform metrics (ROAS, CPA by campaign). Strong ones report business metrics: blended CAC, marginal CAC at current spend tier, cohort payback, intake/checkout conversion. If reporting doesn't include the funnel and attribution layer, the agency is only accountable for the part of the system easiest to make look good.

## 8. "What do you need from us to succeed?"

A revealing inversion. Generic shops say "just access and budget" because dependency is the retainer's moat. Specialists hand you a list: claim substantiation docs, provider availability for creative, product/offer flexibility, fast lander deploy access. An agency that asks nothing of you is planning to operate at the surface of your business.

## 9. "Why shouldn't we hire you?"

Any agency worth hiring has a real answer, because specialists have edges — and edges have shapes. Ours: we only work in health, telehealth, and supplements, so if you're a fashion brand we're the wrong call; and we build systems that take ~31 days to compound, so if you need a miracle by Friday, we'll say so on the call.

## The pattern behind all nine

Every question is measuring the same thing: **does this agency have category-specific systems, or category-agnostic promises?** Health is a regulated, claim-constrained, trust-driven vertical. The playbooks that scale a streetwear brand will get a telehealth account banned.
