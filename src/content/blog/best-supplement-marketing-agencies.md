Supplement marketing agencies range from specialists that pair claim-safe paid ads with Klaviyo retention, to performance creative studios, to broader DTC agencies with Amazon teams. Each agency below shows supplement work on its own website. Choose by where you sell (Shopify, Amazon or practitioners) and by how each agency handles FDA and FTC claim rules.

A supplement brand lives or dies on two things an agency controls: how fast it can produce creative that still says nothing a regulator or ad reviewer will object to, and whether the second and third orders arrive. This guide lists the agencies that publish supplement work, quotes what each says about itself, and gives you the supplement-specific questions to ask.

## How did we pick the agencies on this list?

We collected the agencies that showed up repeatedly in October 2026 searches and AI answers for "supplement marketing agency" and "best supplement marketing agencies", then went to each agency's own website. To make the list, an agency had to publish supplement-specific evidence there: a supplement service page, a supplement case study, or its own account of supplement work. Agencies we couldn't verify that way are not included, however often they appear in other lists.

Each table cell quotes or paraphrases the agency's own page, with a link; we read all of them on Oct 30, 2026. "Not stated" means we looked and the page didn't say. Nothing here grades an agency's quality, results or prices, none of the agencies was contacted, and no one paid for a place.

## Which type of agency fits how you sell?

Where your revenue comes from decides which agency type fits better than any ranking could:

- **DTC brands on Shopify with subscriptions:** the work is Meta and TikTok creative at volume, plus Klaviyo flows that turn a first order into a subscription. Forge Digital Marketing and Top Growth Marketing both describe paid ads paired with Klaviyo retention on their pages; Y'all leads with performance creative and UGC; Sweat Pants Agency leads with email and SMS; PELORA covers both creative and email.
- **Amazon-led or Amazon-plus-DTC brands:** you need listing copy, A+ content and Amazon Ads run as one funnel with your site. On the pages we read, PELORA names Amazon and Shopify together, and ATTN's supplement case study includes an Amazon storefront.
- **Practitioner-channel brands** (sold through clinicians or practitioner-only lines): none of the agencies above describes practitioner-channel marketing on the pages we read. If that's your model, ask for that experience directly before you shortlist anyone.

## What should you check that is specific to supplements?

Start with the general questions for any health growth agency, which we keep on our [generalist DTC agency vs health specialist comparison](/compare/generalist-dtc-agency-alternative/). Then add the supplement-only checks:

1. **Claim substantiation, in writing.** Under DSHEA, a supplement making a structure/function claim must have substantiation, notify FDA within 30 days of marketing the claim, and carry the disclaimer that FDA has not evaluated it ([FDA on structure/function claims](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/structurefunction-claims)); the FTC expects competent and reliable scientific evidence behind every ad claim ([FTC health products guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)). Ask who on the agency's team signs off claims and where the substantiation file lives.
2. **Meta's 2026 rules.** Every supplement ad on Meta must target 18+, cure claims are banned for a closed list of diseases, and clickbait outcome promises without qualifiers are out. Since July 2026 the written weight-loss side-by-side ban is gone. Ask how the agency changed its creative after that revision ([supplements on Meta](/advertising/supplements-on-meta/)).
3. **TikTok's market-by-market rules.** Since September 2026, TikTok lists weight loss supplements as possible in the US and Canada for 18+ with healthy-lifestyle framing, and only in select UK and EU countries ([supplements on TikTok](/advertising/supplements-on-tiktok/)). Ask which markets they'd launch in and why.
4. **Google's unapproved substances list.** Ephedra, hCG for weight loss, DHEA outside the US and claims of prescription-drug equivalence get disapproved ([supplements on Google](/advertising/supplements-on-google/)). Ask them to check your ingredient list against it before you spend.
5. **Retention, not just first orders.** Ask for the agency's plan for orders two and three, because subscription economics set how much you can pay for the first. Our [supplement CAC playbook](/blog/supplement-brand-lower-cac-playbook/) shows the math.

### How do supplement marketing agencies price their work?

Three agencies above publish pricing terms on the pages we read. PELORA says "Retainers start at $3,500 a month with a three-month minimum", with ad spend billed to the brand directly and not marked up. Top Growth Marketing says health and wellness agency fees "typically range from $2,000–$10,000/month depending on revenue tier and channel scope" and that it works month-to-month. ATTN's homepage says "No long-term contracts". The others don't state prices, so ask for the fee model and minimum term in writing.

### Do supplement ads need any certification?

Not for ordinary dietary supplements. Neither Meta's nor Google's policy names a certification for them; both name one for CBD. What supplement ads do need is the age, claim and ingredient rules above.
