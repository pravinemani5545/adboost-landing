LegitScript certification costs $975 per website to apply plus $2,150 a year once approved, according to LegitScript's published fees. Meta requires it for telehealth providers and online pharmacies that promote prescription drugs, and Google requires it before its own certification for telemedicine prescription services. General telehealth promotion on Meta needs no certification.

If you've watched a telehealth ad account get suspended on day one, a missing [LegitScript certification](/glossary/legitscript-certification/) is one of the most common reasons, and it's avoidable if you plan for it. Here's what it is, who needs it, what it costs, how long it takes, and how to sequence it so it doesn't cost you a launch window.

*Certification fees and platform rules change. Fees below were checked on LegitScript's site, and platform rules against Meta's, Google's and TikTok's live policies, on Oct 30, 2026; confirm both before you budget. This is practitioner guidance, not legal advice.*

## What is LegitScript certification?

LegitScript certification is a **third-party accreditation that verifies an online pharmacy or telehealth provider operates legally.** Ad platforms and payment processors rely on it so they don't have to vet every healthcare advertiser themselves. It does not replace the platforms' own rules: Meta still requires its own authorization, and Google its own certification, on top of it.

## Which ad platforms require LegitScript certification?

Meta and Google require it for prescription telehealth and online pharmacy advertising. TikTok's policies do not name telehealth at all. What each platform's own policy says:

| Platform | When LegitScript is required | What else you need |
|---|---|---|
| Meta | "Meta requires online pharmacies and telehealth providers to be actively certified with LegitScript" to promote prescription drugs. Ads that "Promote telehealth services generally" need no written authorization | Meta authorization, targeting only the US, Canada or New Zealand, 18+ ([Meta Drugs and Pharmaceuticals](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/)) |
| Google | Telemedicine providers offering prescription drug services must be "accredited by LegitScript's Healthcare Merchant Certification Program" | Google's own certification as well ([Google healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)) |
| TikTok | Not named for telehealth. US online pharmacy ads need proof of certification from a third-party body "such as the National Association of Boards of Pharmacy (NABP) or LegitScript, or regional equivalent" | Prescription drug ads may be allowed in the US and Canada only with regulator approval and 18+ ([TikTok Healthcare and Pharmaceuticals](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-healthcare-pharmaceuticals)) |

## Who actually needs LegitScript certification?

You need it if you advertise:

- **Telehealth that prescribes** (including GLP-1, TRT, mental health, dermatology) and want to promote the prescription side on Meta or Google.
- **Online pharmacies** and drug sellers.
- **CBD:** Meta asks for LegitScript certification for CBD ads, and TikTok's US policy asks for it for topical hemp products.

LegitScript also runs a separate certification program for behavioral health and substance use disorder providers.

You generally **don't** need it for general telehealth promotion on Meta, for education about prescription drugs, or for ordinary dietary supplements: neither Meta nor Google names a certification for supplements ([Google's unapproved substances policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595718) names one only for CBD). Running general telehealth ads while certification is in process is the main lever for brands that want to stay live on Meta. See how that plays out for [telehealth on Meta](/advertising/telehealth-on-meta/).

## How much does LegitScript certification cost?

LegitScript publishes its healthcare certification fees on its [certification page](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/) (checked Oct 30, 2026):

| Fee | Amount | When you pay it |
|---|---|---|
| Application fee | $975 per website, nonrefundable | When you apply |
| Expedited processing (optional) | $2,500 per application | At application; review starts within 2 business days of submission |
| Annual certification fee | $2,150 per website | Once approved, then every year |
| Annual fee under probationary certification | $3,995 per website | Only if LegitScript places you on heightened monitoring |

For one website on standard certification, that is $3,125 in the first year and $2,150 a year after. Each domain needs its own application and annual fee. LegitScript points some business models to its enterprise certification instead, including digital health platforms that contract with multiple pharmacies or provider networks, telemedicine providers contracting with numerous medical professionals, and med spas with one URL but many locations.

## How long does LegitScript certification take?

LegitScript does not publish a standard review time on its certification or FAQ pages. What it does publish: paying the $2,500 expedited fee starts the review within 2 business days of submission. In our experience, plan on 4–8 weeks from application to approval, sometimes longer, and most of the delay sits in the paperwork.

The process itself: application → licensing and ownership documentation → due-diligence review → approval → ongoing monitoring and annual renewal. LegitScript publishes [application checklists by business type and region](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/), so gather your licensing and corporate documents before you apply.

## How do you check whether a website is LegitScript-certified?

Use LegitScript's [website certification status tool](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/website-certification-status/) to look up a domain. Check your own domains once you're approved, and check any pharmacy or telehealth partner whose fulfillment carries your brand, because Meta's rule is that providers be "actively certified", not certified once.

## Is LegitScript the same as Google's own certification?

No. On Google, LegitScript comes first and Google's own Prescription drug services certification second; the steps, and Google's 2026 market changes for telemedicine, are on our [telehealth on Google](/advertising/telehealth-on-google/) page. On Meta, the pair you need is LegitScript plus Meta's authorization.

## How to sequence certification so it doesn't cost you a launch

The mistake we see most: brands treat certification as something to figure out *after* they build campaigns, then lose weeks of runway waiting. Do it the other way around:

1. **Start LegitScript before you build anything else.** It's the long pole in the tent.
2. **While it's in process, run compliant education and brand-awareness ads** that don't name a drug or promote prescribing, so you're building audiences and pixels the whole time.
3. **Get your provider network and state licensing squared away in parallel.** You can only treat patients where you have a licensed provider.
4. **Then complete each platform's own step** (Google's certification, Meta's authorization) and flip on the Rx campaigns.

Done this way, certification runs in the background instead of blocking your launch.

## FAQ

### Does LegitScript certification guarantee my ads will be approved?

No. It is the entry ticket, not approval. Meta still requires its own authorization and limits prescription drug ads to the US, Canada and New Zealand and people 18 or older, and Google requires its own certification after LegitScript. Each ad is still reviewed against the platform's content rules.

### Can a telehealth brand run Meta ads before it is certified?

Yes, within limits. Meta's Drugs and Pharmaceuticals standard says ads that "Promote telehealth services generally" or educate about prescription drugs need no written authorization. Naming or promoting the prescription drug is what requires LegitScript and Meta's authorization.

### Do supplement brands need LegitScript certification?

Not for ordinary dietary supplements: neither Meta's nor Google's policy names a certification for them. CBD is the exception, where Meta asks for LegitScript certification.
