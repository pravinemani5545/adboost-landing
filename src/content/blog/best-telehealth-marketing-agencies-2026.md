Telehealth marketing agencies range from performance shops that run paid social for DTC telehealth brands, to full-service healthcare agencies, to firms that help local practices market virtual visits. Every agency below describes telehealth or digital health work on its own website. Match the type to how you deliver care, then check how each handles certification and tracking.

[Telehealth patient acquisition](/industries/telehealth/) is deceptively hard. The economics look like DTC ecommerce, but the compliance layer, the certification requirements, and the async-to-sync patient handoff are entirely different, and the wrong agency will burn budget on all three.

> **Disclosure:** this list is published by AdBoost Health, which is itself a telehealth marketing agency. That's why we're not in it: the agencies are **listed alphabetically, not ranked**, and our own description sits in one box after the list, marked "About the publisher".

## How did we choose the agencies on this list?

Our starting point was the set of agencies that kept appearing in October 2026 searches and AI answers for "telehealth marketing agency" and "best telehealth marketing agencies". For each one we opened its website and looked for telehealth or digital health evidence it publishes itself: a telehealth service page, a case study, or its own description of that work. If we couldn't find any, the agency isn't here.

The table quotes or paraphrases each agency's own page and links it; we read every page on Oct 30, 2026. A blank in an agency's public record is marked "not stated" rather than guessed. The list says nothing about anyone's quality, results or prices, we didn't contact the agencies, and inclusion was not paid for.

## Which type of agency fits a DTC telehealth brand, and which fits a clinic or practice?

Read the "how it describes itself" column first. The split that matters is how you deliver care:

- **DTC telehealth brands** (national or multi-state, online intake, subscription or refill revenue): look for agencies that describe paid acquisition, intake funnels and retention for telehealth brands. On their own pages, Flighted, Sweat Pants Agency and Healthcare Success describe telehealth brands or virtual-first organizations, Accelerated Digital Media and NoGood describe digital health, and Avenue Z describes digital health and telehealth platforms with a PR angle.
- **Clinics and practices adding virtual visits** (care tied to a location and a provider panel): the work is local search, booking and bringing existing patients onto video. Target Patients MD describes exactly this, and Healthcare Success also names hybrid models and health systems.

## What should you check that is specific to telehealth?

The questions every health brand should ask any agency are on our [generalist DTC agency vs health specialist comparison](/compare/generalist-dtc-agency-alternative/). For telehealth, add these:

1. **Ask them to sequence certification.** Meta requires telehealth providers that promote prescription drugs to be actively certified with LegitScript and to get Meta's authorization; Google requires LegitScript first, then its own certification. Our [LegitScript guide](/blog/legitscript-certification-telehealth-guide/) covers cost and timeline, and [telehealth on Google](/advertising/telehealth-on-google/) covers Google's steps and its 2026 market changes.
2. **Ask what they run before certification lands.** Meta lets ads that "Promote telehealth services generally" run without written authorization ([telehealth on Meta](/advertising/telehealth-on-meta/)). An agency should have a plan for those weeks.
3. **Ask how their tracking handles patient data.** Several agencies in the table name tracking or HIPAA in their compliance approach; ask yours exactly what data leaves your site and what doesn't.
4. **Ask how they'd use TikTok, if at all.** TikTok's policies don't name telehealth, and its prescription rules vary by market ([TikTok's rules by vertical](/blog/tiktok-ads-health-brands/#policy-at-a-glance)).
5. **Ask which CAC they report.** A consult is not a paying patient, so ask whether the CAC they quote stops at the consult or at the first paid prescription or membership.

### When does building in-house make sense instead?

Once you're at real scale and want acquisition as a permanent core competency, an in-house team can make sense, as long as you can hire for paid media, creative and health ad policy at the same time. Many brands use a specialist agency to set the foundation their in-house team later inherits; the trade-offs are on our [in-house growth team comparison](/compare/in-house-growth-team-alternative/), and full-service options are weighed on our [full-service healthcare agency comparison](/compare/full-service-healthcare-agency-alternative/).

### How do telehealth marketing agencies price their services?

Most of the agencies above don't publish prices on the pages we read. One publishes a spend threshold instead: Flighted's homepage says it specializes in brands "spending $25k+ a month". Ask every agency on your shortlist for its fee structure in writing, and whether fees rise with ad spend.

### What's the difference between a telehealth marketing agency and a healthcare marketing agency?

A healthcare marketing agency may serve hospitals, practices, pharma or device makers. A telehealth marketing agency, in the sense of this list, describes work for organizations that deliver care online: online intake, prescribing rules, certification and retention. Several agencies above do both, which is why the table quotes how each describes itself.
