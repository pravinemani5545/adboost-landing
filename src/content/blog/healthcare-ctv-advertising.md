Most health brands reach the same point eventually. Search is capturing the demand that exists, social is creating some more, and both are getting more expensive at the margin. Someone on the team suggests TV, usually meaning connected TV: streaming ads bought digitally, targeted by geography and audience rather than by broadcast daypart.

The pitch for healthcare CTV advertising is real: streaming is close to half of US TV viewing, the ads run full screen with sound on, and they can be bought in small increments. So is the risk: CTV is the easiest channel to spend on and the hardest to prove. Add it too early, or without a way to measure it, and you get a line item nobody can defend.

This post covers where CTV and programmatic actually fit in a healthcare media plan: what they are, what they can and cannot do that search and social don't, how health targeting rules change the buy, how to measure it honestly, and the readiness test we use before recommending a first dollar.

## What is healthcare CTV advertising?

Healthcare CTV advertising is healthcare video advertising that runs on internet-connected TV screens: smart TVs, streaming sticks, and game consoles, inside streaming apps and services. It looks like a TV commercial to the viewer but is bought, targeted, and reported like digital media.

A few terms get used interchangeably, so it helps to separate them:

- **CTV (connected TV)** describes the screen: a television connected to the internet.
- **OTT (over-the-top)** describes the delivery: video streamed over the internet instead of through cable or satellite. OTT ads can run on a TV, phone, or laptop. In practice, most OTT healthcare advertising budgets are aimed at the TV screen.
- **Programmatic** describes the buying method: ad inventory purchased through software, usually a demand-side platform, with auction or pre-negotiated deals instead of insertion orders and phone calls. Healthcare programmatic advertising covers CTV, but also display, online video, audio, and digital out-of-home.

So "connected TV healthcare" campaigns are usually programmatic, but not all programmatic is CTV, and a display retargeting line does a very different job from a 30-second streaming spot.

## Why are health brands looking past search and social?

Because search and social have structural ceilings, and CTV reaches people where those channels don't.

Search can only capture demand that already exists, so past a point more spend buys weaker queries at higher cost (see our guide to [scaling healthcare paid search](/blog/healthcare-paid-search-scaling/)). Social creates demand, but in a crowded feed, often with sound off.

Meanwhile, attention has moved. Nielsen reported that streaming reached [49.0% of total TV viewing in July 2026](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2026/tv-usage-kicks-usual-summer-slowdown-fueled-by-world-cup-and-streaming-in-nielsens-july-gauge-reports/), with YouTube alone at a record 14.2% of TV. That is a large share of evening attention that search and feed-based social do not touch, delivered on the biggest screen in the house.

For health brands, that combination matters for three reasons:

- **Considered decisions need explanation.** Hormone therapy, weight management, mental health care, and fertility treatment are not impulse purchases. A 30-second spot with sound has room to explain a mechanism, introduce a clinician, or address a common objection in a way a six-second feed scroll does not.
- **It feeds the channels you already run.** A patient who saw your spot on the couch rarely converts on the TV. They search your brand name later, or recognize your ad in a feed. Done right, CTV makes search and social cheaper, which is also why it is so hard to measure.

## Where does CTV fit in a healthcare media plan?

Healthcare CTV advertising is a demand-creation channel that sits above search and social, not a replacement for either. Its job is to build awareness and consideration in defined markets so that search captures more branded demand and social retargeting converts a warmer audience.

Think of a healthcare media strategy in three layers:

1. **Capture**: search (brand and non-brand), plus retargeting. Converts people already looking.
2. **Create and convert**: paid social and short-form video. Generates new demand and often converts it in the same channel.
3. **Create at scale**: CTV, online video, and broader programmatic. Builds reach and familiarity across a market, with conversion happening later through the first two layers.

The order matters. CTV works best on top of a capture layer that is already efficient, because a meaningful share of the response it generates arrives as branded searches and direct visits. If your brand search campaign is weak or your landing pages leak, CTV pours new interest into a funnel that loses it. That is why, in our [scaling playbook](/blog/scale-health-brand-50k-500k/), YouTube and CTV come last in the channel sequence: real reach, but only after the cheaper layers are running well.

The exception is a regional provider or clinic group with a tight service area, where geography makes targeting and measurement simple enough to justify CTV earlier.

## How does targeting work under healthcare privacy rules?

More narrowly than vendor decks suggest. Targeting people on CTV based on their health conditions is restricted on major platforms and legally risky everywhere, so most health brands should plan on geography, demographics, and context, with creative doing the qualifying.

Three constraints shape the buy:

- **Platform policy.** Google lists health as a sensitive interest category in its [restricted targeting rules for personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465?hl=en). Advertisers promoting health products and services cannot use advertiser-curated audiences (such as uploaded customer lists or lookalikes), though predefined Google audiences like demographics, life events, affinity, and location remain available. That applies to YouTube campaigns served on TV screens just as it does to mobile.
- **Federal privacy rules.** HIPAA covered entities cannot send patient information to ad tech vendors, and that includes using patient lists to build CTV audiences without proper authorization. Non-HIPAA health brands are not exempt from scrutiny: the FTC's [updated Health Breach Notification Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/04/updated-ftc-health-breach-notification-rule-puts-new-provisions-place-protect-users-health-apps) explicitly covers health apps and connected services, and followed enforcement actions over health data shared through tracking pixels.
- **State law.** Some states go further. Washington's My Health My Data Act [prohibits geofences around in-person health care facilities](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.080) when used to track people seeking care or send them health-related ads. Location-based programmatic tactics that are routine in retail can be illegal in health.

Some vendors sell "health audiences" built from third-party data, often aimed at pharma. Before buying one, get a written answer on where the data came from and whether any of it derives from medical records or health-app activity. If the answer is vague, don't buy it.

What works instead:

- **Geographic targeting** at the state, metro, or zip-cluster level, matched to where you are licensed, have locations, or have capacity.
- **Demographic targeting** by age range and household signals that match your patient profile, without inferring conditions.
- **Contextual and content targeting**: health, wellness, fitness, or news programming where the audience self-selects.
- **Creative as the qualifier.** The spot names the problem clearly enough that the right viewers lean in and everyone else ignores it. This is the same principle that makes compliant [healthcare ad creative](/blog/healthcare-ad-creative-strategy/) work on social.

None of this is legal advice. If your targeting plan involves any patient data, audience upload, or location tactic, run it past counsel before launch.

## How do you buy CTV as a health brand?

There are three common entry points, and the right one depends on budget and how much control you need.

- **YouTube on TV screens through Google Ads.** The lowest-friction start for brands already running Google. Campaigns can include TV screens in device targeting, and Google supports [QR codes on connected TV ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16903036?hl=en) in eligible formats, so viewers can respond from the couch. The health targeting limits above still apply.
- **Programmatic through a demand-side platform.** Inventory across many streaming apps, more control over frequency and supply, and more measurement integrations, plus more risk of paying for low-quality supply if nobody checks where ads ran.
- **Direct with streaming publishers.** Buying from a streaming service's own ad platform or sales team. Useful for premium placements and specific audiences, usually with higher minimums.

Whichever route you choose, insist on placement-level transparency: which apps and publishers your ads ran on, at what frequency. Programmatic CTV supply quality varies, and a cheap CPM on inventory nobody watches is not a deal.

## What creative does healthcare CTV need?

TV-screen creative has different rules than feed creative, and recycling a vertical social ad onto a 65-inch screen rarely works.

- **Horizontal, sound-on, and built for 15 or 30 seconds.** Viewers are leaning back, not scrolling. You have time for a structure: problem, how care works, who provides it, what to do next.
- **One memorable next step.** There is no click, so use a brand name to search, a short URL, or a QR code where supported.
- **Brand name early and often.** Most response arrives later through search, so say it and show it.
- **Same compliance standard as everything else.** No outcome guarantees, no before-and-after implications, clear eligibility language where it applies, and claims your medical reviewer has signed off on. A TV screen makes a non-compliant claim more visible, not less.

A first test does not need a film crew, but it does need properly shot horizontal footage, clean audio, and two or three versions to compare.

## How do you measure CTV performance in healthcare?

Primarily by what happens to your other channels and your blended numbers in the markets where CTV runs, not by what the CTV platform reports. CTV is an assist channel, and its own last-touch reporting will always understate it while view-through attribution will often overstate it.

The industry is still standardizing the basics: the IAB published a [Standardized Measurement Guide for CTV](https://www.iab.com/guidelines/standardized-measurement-guide-for-ctv/) in December 2025 because definitions of impressions, reach, and frequency remain inconsistent across platforms. If counting an impression is still unsettled, a platform's "conversions" number deserves skepticism.

A measurement plan that holds up:

- **Delivery metrics as hygiene.** Household reach, frequency, completion rate, and placement reports show the ads ran properly, not that they worked.
- **Leading indicators.** Branded search volume, direct traffic, and QR scans in flighted markets compared with unflighted ones. Branded search lift is usually the earliest honest signal.
- **"How did you hear about us?"** A free-text survey at booking catches TV mentions no pixel will, with no tracking at all. Our [healthcare marketing attribution guide](/blog/healthcare-marketing-attribution/) covers how surveys fit alongside the rest of the stack.
- **Geo holdouts for the verdict.** Run CTV in matched test markets, hold it out in comparable control markets, and compare new patients and blended CAC from your own CRM or EHR. Geography is CTV's natural unit, which makes it one of the easier channels to test this way. The full test design is in our post on [healthcare incrementality](/blog/healthcare-incrementality/).

Vendor tools that match TV exposure to site visits by household IP or device graph can be directionally useful, but their tags follow the same PHI rules as any other. Nothing from intake, eligibility, or booking pages should flow back to an ad vendor.

## How much should a health brand spend to test CTV?

Enough to reach meaningful frequency in the test markets for long enough to cover your consideration cycle, and no more. CTV tests fail more often from being spread too thin than from being too small.

Here is how to size one, using round, illustrative numbers rather than benchmarks:

- Say your test markets contain 400,000 target households, and you want to reach half of them at an average frequency of about six over eight weeks.
- That is 200,000 households × 6 impressions = 1.2 million impressions.
- At an illustrative $35 CPM, that is roughly $42,000 for the flight, before production.

Then sanity-check it against your economics. If your [patient acquisition cost](/blog/patient-acquisition-cost/) target is $300, the test has to plausibly drive about 140 extra patients across the test markets to break even on media alone. Maybe it will, maybe it won't, but you should know the bar before launch. Our [healthcare marketing forecasting](/blog/healthcare-marketing-forecasting/) guide shows how to plan new channels in spend tiers so a test fits inside a budget finance already approved.

Two sizing rules: concentrate budget in fewer markets rather than going national thin, and commit to the full flight. CTV effects build over weeks, so pausing after ten days means the test never had a chance.

## Is your healthcare brand ready for CTV?

Answer yes to most of these before spending a dollar:

1. **Search is efficient and has a brand campaign**, so the branded demand CTV creates gets captured cheaply, not by competitors.
2. **The landing page and intake flow convert.** Colder traffic exposes funnel leaks fast.
3. **You can count new patients by market** from your own systems. Without that, no holdout can be read.
4. **You have capacity in the target markets**: licensed states, open slots, staff for more inquiries.
5. **You have horizontal, sound-on creative** that has passed compliance review.
6. **Search and social are hitting diminishing returns.** If marginal CAC is still flat, the cheapest growth is still there.

If you are missing two or more, fix those first. CTV amplifies whatever system it sits on top of, including its problems.

## FAQ

### What is healthcare CTV advertising?

It is video advertising for health brands that runs on internet-connected TV screens, inside streaming apps and services. It looks like a TV commercial to the viewer, but it is bought, targeted, and reported like digital media, usually through programmatic platforms, Google Ads, or streaming publishers directly.

### What is the difference between CTV, OTT, and programmatic advertising in healthcare?

CTV describes the screen, a television connected to the internet. OTT describes the delivery, video streamed over the internet, which can play on a TV, phone, or laptop. Programmatic describes the buying method, software-based buying through a demand-side platform. Healthcare programmatic advertising also covers display, online video, audio, and digital out-of-home.

### Can health brands target patients by condition on connected TV?

Generally, no. Targeting people based on health conditions is restricted on major platforms and legally risky everywhere. Google bars health advertisers from advertiser-curated audiences, HIPAA blocks sharing patient data with ad tech vendors, and some states restrict location tactics. Most health brands should target by geography, demographics, and context, and let the creative qualify the viewer.

### How do you measure healthcare CTV advertising?

Measure it by what happens to branded search, direct traffic, survey responses, and blended CAC in the markets where it runs, not by the CTV platform's own reporting. Geo holdouts give the clearest verdict: run CTV in test markets, hold it out in comparable control markets, and compare new patients from your own CRM or EHR.

### When is a health brand ready for CTV?

A health brand is ready when search is efficient and includes a brand campaign, the landing page and intake flow convert, new patients can be counted by market, there is capacity in the target markets, and compliant horizontal creative exists. Search and social should also be hitting diminishing returns. Missing two or more of these means fix them first.

## The bottom line on healthcare CTV advertising

CTV and programmatic can be a legitimate growth layer for health brands once search and social are efficient, the funnel converts, and you can measure patients by market. It is a demand-creation channel, so judge it by what it does to branded search, survey responses, and blended CAC in test markets, not by its own dashboard. Target by geography, demographics, and context, let creative qualify the viewer, and keep patient data out of the ad stack entirely.
