If you run paid social for a [GLP-1 or weight-loss brand](/industries/glp-1-weight-loss/), the ground moved under you twice. In December 2025, a bipartisan coalition of **35+ state attorneys general** publicly pressured Meta to crack down on misleading, AI-generated weight-loss drug ads (per the coalition's December 2025 letter to Meta), and the FDA issued warning letters to telehealth companies over compounded GLP-1 marketing (per the FDA's published warning-letter database). Then, in July 2026, Meta rewrote the written rules themselves. The brands still scaling are the ones that treated compliance as the strategy, not the afterthought.

Here's what actually changed, what didn't, and the exact way compliant GLP-1 brands keep their Meta accounts live in 2026.

*Ad policies and enforcement change frequently. The Meta rules on this page were checked against Meta's live ad standards on Oct 30, 2026; verify current requirements before launch. This is practitioner guidance, not legal advice.*

## What did the 35-state-AG action actually change?

The short version: **the AG pressure changed enforcement, and Meta's July 2026 revision then changed the written rules.** Until July 2026, Meta's Health and Wellness ad standard already banned side-by-side weight-loss comparisons and ads that imply "negative self-perception". What the AG pressure changed first is how aggressively those rules were enforced, especially against AI-generated testimonials and misleading claims. Accounts that skated by on borderline creative in early 2025 got flagged.

Two things are worth internalizing:

- **AI-generated fake testimonials are now an active enforcement target.** The AGs specifically called out AI-fueled weight-loss ads. If your creative uses synthetic "patients" making outcome claims, that's exactly what's under the microscope.
- **The compounded-GLP-1 window has closed.** With the FDA shortages resolved (tirzepatide in December 2024, semaglutide in February 2025) and 503A/503B enforcement deadlines lapsed by mid-2025, mass-market compounded copies are no longer protected. Marketing them as equivalent to Wegovy or Zepbound is an FDA and FTC problem; neither of Meta's ad standards addresses compounded drugs (see [FDA: what telehealth companies should know when promoting compounded drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-telehealth-companies-what-know-when-promoting-compounded-drugs)).

## What did Meta's July 2026 revision change for GLP-1 advertisers?

In July 2026 Meta revised its [Health and Wellness ad standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/), and the change log shows exactly what moved. For GLP-1 brands:

- **Deleted:** the ban on "Side-by-side comparison after the use of a product or transformation for weight loss", and the sentence that ads "must not imply or attempt to generate negative self-perception or declare there is a perfect body type".
- **Added:** weight-loss ads can't contain "statements of inferiority about physical appearance", and health ads can't use clickbait such as "promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers".
- **Not changed:** "For weight loss products that require prescription", Meta still points to its [Drugs and Pharmaceuticals standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/), which last changed in February 2025 and says nothing about imagery.

So the premise many advertisers still repeat, that "the written policy didn't change", is out of date: it changed in July 2026. For non-prescription weight-loss products the imagery rules loosened. For prescription GLP-1 ads, Meta's text is silent on imagery, which is why we still keep transformation creative off them. The full deleted and added text, and how to tell which standard applies to your offer, is in our [summary of Meta's July 2026 change](/advertising/#meta-july-2026).

## Can you still advertise GLP-1 on Meta in 2026?

Yes, with real constraints. Naming the prescription drug (semaglutide, tirzepatide, Wegovy, Zepbound) requires active **LegitScript certification** plus Meta authorization, targeting only the US, Canada, and New Zealand, gated to 18+. Everything about the *creative* is where brands get burned. For the full breakdown, see our page on [advertising GLP-1 on Meta](/advertising/glp-1-on-meta/), but the headline rules as Meta writes them today are:

- **No "statements of inferiority about physical appearance"**: copy that attacks the viewer's body, specific body parts or appearance.
- **No clickbait outcome promises**: "promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers" are out, so "lose X lbs by summer" needs real qualification or, better, a rewrite.
- **No "Close up on specific body area by pinching fat"** in weight-loss creative.
- **No under-18 targeting**, and prescription drug ads only in the US, Canada and New Zealand.

Two more lines we hold that are not Meta's wording: no before/after or side-by-side transformation imagery on prescription GLP-1 offers (Meta's standard for those ads says nothing about imagery, so we treat it as a risk), and no marketing compounded GLP-1 as equivalent to the branded drug (an FDA and FTC issue, not a Meta rule).

## What gets a GLP-1 ad account disabled?

From what we see across accounts, the disablement cascade almost always starts with one of these:

1. Naming the drug without active LegitScript + Meta authorization.
2. Unqualified "lose X lbs" claims, or transformation imagery that ran under the pre-July 2026 side-by-side ban.
3. Copy that attacks the viewer's appearance, which Meta now calls "statements of inferiority about physical appearance".
4. Pixel or landing-page signals that classify you as an uncertified Rx pharmacy (our observation from audits, not Meta's wording).
5. AI-generated testimonials making outcome claims.

The reason this matters so much: a disabled account at $200K/month in spend isn't a creative problem, it's a company problem. The recovery timeline is measured in weeks, and your CAC goes to infinity while you wait.

## How compliant GLP-1 brands keep scaling

The brands winning right now sell **the program and the outcome, not the molecule.** That reframe unlocks almost everything:

- **Lead with metabolic-health education**, not the drug. A "GLP-1 companion program" or "medical weight-loss program" framing keeps you inside policy while still capturing intent.
- **Kill the scale and the before/after on Rx offers.** Replace transformation imagery with single lifestyle shots, disclaimered testimonials, and problem-aware education (appetite, "food noise," the science). This is our recommendation for prescription programs, not a written Meta rule since July 2026.
- **Move the clinical intake off-platform.** The eligibility quiz and prescribing flow live on your site, not in the ad.
- **If you name the drug, get certified.** Complete LegitScript, obtain Meta authorization, geo-restrict to US/CA/NZ, add the consult disclaimer, and gate to 18+.

This isn't a growth tax. Compliant brands consistently hold lower, more stable CAC than the ones playing whack-a-mole with rejections, because their accounts stay live long enough for the algorithm to learn. ## The bottom line

The 35-state-AG action didn't kill GLP-1 advertising on Meta; it killed *lazy* GLP-1 advertising. Meta's July 2026 rewrite then changed the written rules: the side-by-side ban is gone, and appearance-shaming and unqualified outcome promises are now the named problems. AI-faked testimonials and compounded-equivalence claims remain enforcement magnets. Sell the program, keep the drug name off the creative unless you're certified, and move the clinical work off the ad. That's the version of this business that survives the crackdown and scales through it.
